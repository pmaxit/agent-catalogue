    Temporal-Difference Learning

    When a learner must improve estimates before an episode ends, Monte Carlo waits too long. Temporal-difference methods update from partial experience — bootstrapping value from value.

    The credit-assignment problem

    In sequential decision problems, reward often arrives long after the actions that produced it. Assigning credit to earlier states is the central difficulty of prediction and control. Monte Carlo methods resolve the issue by waiting for returns; temporal-difference (TD) methods resolve it by guessing.

    TD methods combine the sampling of Monte Carlo with the bootstrapping of dynamic programming — and need neither a model nor a finished episode.

    TD(0) from the Bellman equation

    The Bellman equation for a state-value function under policy π expresses Vπ(s) in terms of the expected next reward and next state value. Replacing the expectation with a sample and the next-state value with the current estimate yields the TD(0) update:

    V(S_t) ← V(S_t) + α [ R_{t+1} + γ V(S_{t+1}) − V(S_t) ]

    Random walk — worked sketch

    Consider a five-state random walk with absorbing terminals at either end. Starting from the center, every episode is a sequence of left/right steps until absorption. After a few dozen episodes of tabular TD(0), value estimates concentrate on the true probabilities of terminating right — without ever computing full returns.

    Exercise

    Implement tabular TD(0) for the random walk. Plot V after 1, 10, and 100 episodes. What happens when α is too large?
